About.

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. student in Computer Engineering at Wireless@Virginia Tech, advised by Professor Y. Thomas Hou. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027.

I received my M.S. in Computer Engineering from Virginia Tech in 2024 and dual B.S. degrees in Computer Science and Mathematics from the Pennsylvania State University in 2022, graduating with magna cum laude honors.

My work combines RF impairment modeling, link-level performance analysis, transceiver algorithm design, and MAC-layer resource allocation. I am especially interested in channel estimation, synchronization, beamforming, and real-time scheduling.

Research.

Real-Time PHY- and MAC-Layer Resource Allocation for URLLC

  • Develop a multiuser MIMO-OFDM link-level simulator with 3GPP channel models, imperfect channel state information (CSI), RF and PHY nonidealities, channel estimation, beamforming, modulation, and channel coding.
  • Analyze how practical RF impairments and imperfect CSI affect baseband processing, multiuser interference, effective SINR, and spectrum utilization.
  • Use communication-theoretic insights to derive a compact, system-aware feature representation, then process it with a lightweight neural network for rapid scheduling decisions.
  • Perform beamforming, scheduling, and resource allocation across the PHY and MAC layers in under five milliseconds while satisfying 100% of the specified reliability and latency constraints across the evaluated scenarios.
