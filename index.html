My research focuses on PHY/MAC design for wireless communication systems with practical RF awareness. I use communication theory, mathematical optimization, and machine learning to develop reliable, resource-efficient wireless systems.

About.

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. student in Computer Engineering with the Wireless@Virginia Tech research group, advised by Professor Y. Thomas Hou.

I received my M.S. in Computer Engineering from Virginia Tech in 2024 and dual B.S. degrees in Computer Science and Mathematics from the Pennsylvania State University in 2022, graduating with magna cum laude honors.

My expertise spans PHY/MAC-layer modeling and algorithm design, link-level performance analysis, and digital signal processing.

Research.

PHY/MAC Algorithms for Reliable Wireless Systems

  • Design PHY/MAC control and radio-resource management algorithms for multiuser wireless systems, accounting for cellular control procedures, imperfect channel state information, multiuser interference, and transmission-reliability requirements.
  • Develop 3GPP-based multiuser MIMO-OFDM link-level simulations incorporating channel-estimation uncertainty, transmit beamforming and precoding, modulation, channel coding and decoding, and link-performance evaluation.
  • Design real-time machine-learning-assisted beamforming and MAC scheduling algorithms under practical PHY/MAC constraints, including compact neural networks for SINR ranking and transmission-feasibility classification.
  • Apply communication theory, real-time GPU algorithms, convex optimization, machine learning, and reinforcement learning to PHY/MAC resource allocation under sub-5-ms execution-time constraints.
  • Analyze how RF impairments and transceiver nonidealities affect baseband processing, multiuser interference, effective SINR, and spectrum utilization.

Projects.

MIMO-OFDM Link-Level Simulator

  • Built a MATLAB link-level simulator with AWGN and time- and frequency-selective multipath channels, including Rayleigh, Rician, and Nakagami-m fading, delay and Doppler spread, and spatial antenna correlation.
  • Implemented OFDM processing, pilot-based channel estimation, equalization, coding, adaptive subcarrier bit loading, and carrier-frequency and symbol-timing synchronization.
  • Implemented MRT, MRC, SVD-based spatial multiplexing, Alamouti coding, and ZF, MMSE, SIC, and maximum-likelihood detection; evaluated BER, BLER, throughput, spectral efficiency, and pilot-overhead trade-offs.

Parallel Computing for Radio-Resource Management

  • Implemented a parallel MAC-layer resource-allocation algorithm in C/C++ using OpenMP, MPI, and CUDA, with shared-memory flags and block-level synchronization for thread coordination.
  • Explored CPU memory locality through loop reordering, matrix transposition, and cache tiling.

Reinforcement Learning

  • Implemented and evaluated TD(λ), Q-learning, policy-gradient and actor-critic methods, PPO, and PPO-λ in Python and PyTorch, using tabular, Fourier-basis, and neural-network approximations.
  • Tested algorithms in Cliff Walking, CartPole, and Continuous Mountain Car; tuned hyperparameters with Optuna and compared convergence, episodic reward, and training stability.

End-to-End 2 × 2 MIMO RAN Prototype (Team Project)

  • Designed and implemented a QoS-aware MAC scheduler that allocates radio resources using application throughput and latency requirements and real-time RAN feedback.
  • Evaluated throughput, latency, and packet loss on the end-to-end prototype.

Software-Defined Cellular Network Deployment and Control

  • Deployed an end-to-end cellular system on the NSF Open AI Cellular platform and verified connectivity across the core, base station, user equipment, and virtual radio link.
  • Connected an O-RAN near-real-time RAN Intelligent Controller to an srsRAN base station and experimented with xApps for KPI monitoring, radio-resource scheduling, and AI-driven network control.
